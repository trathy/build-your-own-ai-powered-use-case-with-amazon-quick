## Step 1: Identify the central question

Every good use case answers one question that a senior leader would ask. Frame yours:

| Focus area | Example central question |
|------------|------------------------|
| Supply chain | "Why are we missing delivery targets at our largest customer, and what is the financial exposure?" |
| Sales | "Which accounts are at risk of churn and what can we do this quarter to save them?" |
| Operations | "Which facility is underperforming and what is causing it?" |
| Finance | "Where are we over budget and what is driving the variance?" |
| Marketing | "Which campaigns are delivering ROI and which should we cut?" |


## Step 2: Define 4-5 key metrics

Pick metrics that:
- Your personas care about
- Can be ranked across entities (locations, accounts, products, regions)
- Have a meaningful trend over time
- Connect to business value (dollars, throughput, risk)

## Step 3: Design the story arc

The best demos follow a four-beat narrative:

| Beat | What happens | Yours |
|------|-------------|-------|
| **H1: The Overview** | Rank your entities by the central metric | _______________ |
| **H2: The Why** | Trace the worst performer to a root cause | _______________ |
| **H3: The Action** | Produce something usable (briefing, report, plan) | _______________ |
| **H4: The Business Case** | Convert the insight into dollar impact | _______________ |

## Step 4: Identify one thing it cannot answer

Pick one question your data will deliberately not cover:

```
Question we cannot answer: _______________
```

This builds credibility. A tool that always has an answer is a tool nobody trusts.
